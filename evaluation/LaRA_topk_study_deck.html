Why these two tasks, why comp = 2
This reuses last month's chunk-count analysis
From the earlier "which questions really stress RAG" analysis of all 2,326 LaRA questions: location and hallu always resolve from a single segment (context_order), and comp is the only task with a multi-segment field (comp_parts) — and it is always exactly 2 segments. LaRA has no question needing 3+ segments.
Retrieving more than 2 chunks for comp can't help it find a segment it doesn't need, so top_k = 2 isn't a simplification for comp — it's the honest ceiling of what the task asks for.
Reasoning is the interesting one instead: most reasoning questions are also single-segment, but a real subset is tagged context_order == "full" — the answer requires synthesizing the whole document, not one chunk. That's the genuine "does more retrieval depth help" question, and it's why reasoning (not comp) is the one we sweep.

Question curation — not first-N
15 per cell, picked to actually stress RAG

Comp — ranked by relative spread

  • relative spread = |a−b| / (segments−1) from comp_parts — how far apart the 2 needed chunks sit in the document.
  • Highest-spread questions kept first per doc type — the closest thing to "chunks from all over the doc" that 32k documents (~3 segments each) offer.
  • book: spread 0.50 (max available) · financial: 0.50–0.67 · paper: up to 1.00 (opposite ends of the doc)

Reasoning — "full"-document questions first

  • Prioritize context_order == "full" (whole-document synthesis) over single-segment questions.
  • book: 15/15 available → used all · financial: 30 available → top 15 used
  • paper: 0 "full" questions exist at 32k — fell back to an even spread across segments 0/1/2 so the set isn't trivially all "first chunk" questions.
Script: select_stress_questions.py, writes curated sets to datasets/query_topk_study/ — the original datasets/query/ files are untouched.

Experiment setup
Scope of this run

Fixed across all cells

  • Generator: qwen2.5:7b · Judge: qwen2.5:14b (decoupled, via Ollama)
  • Context length: 32k only · Chunker: token (chunker-study winner)
  • 3 doc types: book, financial, paper
  • 15 curated questions per (doc type × top_k) cell

Varied

  • reasoning: top_k ∈ {5, 10, 15} — the sweep
  • comp: top_k = 2, fixed (task ceiling, not swept)
  • Judged on both scales: binary True/False and 0–10 numeric
45 reasoning questions × 3 top_k values + 45 comp questions = 180 generations, judged twice (binary + numeric) = 360 judge calls.

Interpretation — the aggregate hides this
The top_k trend depends heavily on doc type

book & financial: real "full"-document questions

  • financial numeric rises cleanly: 52.0 → 57.3 → 59.3 as top_k grows — more chunks genuinely helps when the question needs whole-document synthesis.
  • book is noisier (51.3 → 50.0 → 59.3) — dips at top_k=10 before recovering, most likely n=15 sample noise rather than a real reversal.

paper: no "full" questions — already near ceiling

  • paper reasoning sits at 74–77 numeric / 80–87 binary regardless of top_k — it's the fallback question set (slide 4), not genuinely whole-document, so it doesn't test what this sweep is trying to measure.
  • This is a real limitation of the 32k paper corpus, not a modeling artifact — flagged again on the limitations slide.
The overall numbers on slide 9 are real, but the story is really "more retrieval depth helps when the question actually needs it" — book and financial show that; paper can't, because it has no genuinely multi-chunk reasoning questions to begin with.

Why the scores are low — comp
Retrieval misses, not generation misses
Question (comp · book · top_k=2)

What role does Daisy's mother play in response to Daisy's social interactions with men in both Vevey and Rome?

Groundtruth

Daisy's mother is consistently portrayed as permissive and somewhat unaware, allowing Daisy to freely interact with men in both Vevey and Rome…

Model's answer

“The provided text does not contain any information about Daisy or her mother… The text discusses Alexander Fitzgerald and his aunt Mrs. Fitzgerald but does not mention Daisy or any…”

Numeric judge's reasoning

“Completely irrelevant to the question about Daisy and her mother, instead mentioning a different character from an unspecified text.”

False

Wrong document context, not a wrong answer — the retriever pulled the wrong 2 chunks entirely. 9 of comp's 11 zero-scored answers (11/45 overall) explicitly say some version of “the provided chunks do not contain this information.” Comp's questions were deliberately curated for the widest chunk-to-chunk distance (slide 4), and top_k=2 gives zero margin: miss either one of the 2 needed segments and the model has nothing to work with. This is the retrieval step failing, not the LLM reasoning badly over correct context.

Why the scores are low — reasoning
Some failures are a generation ceiling, not a retrieval one
Question (reasoning · financial · "full")

What was the primary factor contributing to the net increase in total liabilities for Equus Total Return, Inc. from December 31, 2023, to March 31, 2024?

Groundtruth

Borrowing under the margin account, which increased from $44,955,000 (Dec 2023) to $52,970,000 (Mar 2024) to maintain the Fund's RIC status.

Model's answer (top_k=15 — the most chunks it ever got)

“The provided chunks do not contain specific information regarding the primary factors contributing to the net increase in total liabilities… The necessary details are not presented in the given text.”

Same question, same document, 3× more chunks retrieved — still fails. That rules out "not enough retrieval depth" for this failure mode. 14 of 135 reasoning generations (10%) scored ≤1; 11 of those 14 (79%) are this same style of financial question, asking for a computed change between two report dates — the model needs to pull two different numbers from two different filing sections and get the subtraction right. That's a generation/arithmetic limitation of the 7B generator, not a missing-chunk problem — more top_k cannot fix it.

Interpretation
Four takeaways
1
Retrieving more chunks helps reasoning, but modestly — +4.4 points binary and +4.4 points numeric going from top_k=5 to top_k=15 overall, concentrated in doc types that actually have whole-document questions.
2
Comp is bottlenecked elsewhere. Even at its exact ceiling (top_k=2, no missing chunks possible), comp scores 25–27 points below reasoning at every top_k. On the curated highest-relative-spread questions, the model gets the right chunks and still struggles to combine them.
3
The binary/numeric judge gap persists here too — every cell's numeric score sits 6–12 points above its binary accuracy, the same judge-collapse phenomenon flagged in the earlier judge and chunker studies, now visible across top_k as well.
4
Curating "genuinely RAG-stressing" questions (relative spread for comp, "full"-document tag for reasoning) surfaced a real data gap: 32k paper docs have zero whole-document reasoning questions — worth flagging to the professor independently of this study.

Limitations & caveats
What this run does and doesn't show

Scope limits

  • n=15 per cell (180 total generations) — up from the chunker study's n=5, still a bounded run, not the full benchmark.
  • Single generator/judge pair, single chunker (token) held fixed — top_k × chunker interaction is untested.
  • 32k context only; no comparison against 128k, where documents have far more segments and top_k has more room to matter.

The curation itself is a heuristic

  • context_order == "full" is LaRA's own tag for whole-document reasoning questions, not an independently validated difficulty measure.
  • paper's fallback set (no "full" questions available) is not directly comparable to book/financial's genuine "full" sets — see slide 10.
  • Comp's relative-spread ranking is capped by 32k docs having only ~3 segments; a wider spread distinction only shows up at 128k (per the earlier Task A analysis).
